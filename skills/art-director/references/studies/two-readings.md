# Study: one brief, two readings

Open when two projects are about to collapse into the same layout, or when
you are tempted to reuse last week's type pair.

Same facts, two legitimate visual systems. Neither is a theme pack. Do not
paste either onto a client.

## Brief (shared)

Mira Chen publishes field recordings and also cuts a small run of lathe-cut
7-inch records.

Real content:

- Name: Mira Chen
- Latest tape: "Yard at 04:10", 18 min, recorded under the overpass, 2026-08-02
- Also in the catalog: "Ferry wake" (11 min), "Boiler room" (23 min)
- Action: email `mira@fieldcut.example` for remaining lathe-cuts
- Remaining copies of the 7-inch: 14

Primary task: decide whether to listen in the archive or inquire about a
physical cut.

## Reading A — archive

A catalog. Paper, tabular numbers, mono catalog ids. The latest piece is a
row in focus (a playing mark), not a hero billboard. Density is the craft.

[two-readings.archive.html](two-readings.archive.html)

## Reading B — object

The lathe-cut is the proof. A large CSS disc (labeled as a diagram) carries
the title on a paper label. Other titles become a discography. Dark vinyl
field, serif name, no table.

[two-readings.object.html](two-readings.object.html)

## What must not be the same

If you strip the name "Mira Chen" and the gold/red marks, A is still a
catalog and B is still an object on a field. If both files had the same
section order, type ratio, and a centered disc, the study would have failed.

## Takeaway

Context chooses the system. An archive of durations wants rows. A run of 14
objects wants a specimen. Do not "randomize" fonts to fake difference.
