## Task preferences

- **Find an available book quickly:** A has a narrow preference because all six items and aligned availability labels are visible at once. C is close, with stronger search grouping but uneven status positions. B trades immediate collection coverage for more spacious cards.
- **Compare visible book information:** B has a preference for readable title layouts and page counts. C supplies year/category plus author with less scrolling. These images do not show a dedicated details view, so no preference for the actual details step can be established.
- **Reserve for a pickup day on mobile:** B has the clearest efficiency advantage: the complete form and confirmation action fit in the first viewport. A is preferable if immediately visible day alternatives matter more than scrolling, once the user reaches that section. C gives the strongest book context above the form but postpones confirmation.
- **Component and surface consistency:** B is strongest for uniform cover/card construction; C is strongest for the repeated search/form panel treatment. A is restrained and cohesive overall, with a more conspicuous shift to the boxed reservation form.

These are task-specific tradeoffs, not evidence of broad quality superiority. B's mobile efficiency and spacious cards coexist with its substantially longer catalogue. A and C offer better collection coverage; A favors status scanning, while C emphasizes book identity and related surfaces. Six screenshots from one pass cannot establish an overall winner or tested functional quality.
