Screenshots cannot establish successful filtering, submission, cancellation, persistence, keyboard use, or assistive-technology behavior.

## Catalogue and task clarity

Both clearly offer four craft workshops and place category filters before the list. P's warm green palette, quiet headline, large craft illustrations, white card surfaces, and substantial outlined Explore buttons create an approachable studio catalogue. Its mobile illustration strips are narrow enough to crop several objects; titles and descriptions consequently wrap into tall cards. Textile wraps onto a second filter row. Repeated Explore buttons have strong, consistent affordance.

Q has a more emphatic navy typographic voice and a compact editorial list. All five mobile filters fit on one row; intact illustrations and available-session counts help compare options. The mobile catalogue is visibly shorter. Its Explore text links are less visually prominent than P's buttons, and tiny repeated illustration captions add little decision value. On desktop, both show the first row and only part of the second within the supplied viewport; Q's oversized hero and demo banner consume considerable space despite the compact entries.

## Form, hierarchy, and errors

Both present the same understandable sequence: session, number of places, name, reservation action. Their filled submit buttons are unmistakably primary, while navigation back is secondary. P's numbered labels, short introductory instruction, and red name-field border make the form especially explicit. P also clearly places the name helper and validation message below the field, although they add vertical length.

Q retains its stronger brand hierarchy in the detail view, separates duration and supplied materials into a useful two-column facts row, and marks the form with a blue top rule. Its name error is legible below the field, but the field outline itself does not acquire the strong error distinction visible in P. The large workshop headline has more visual weight than the transactional form title; this suits an expressive workshop page but offers less task economy. Both reserve substantial mobile space before the session choices.

## Confirmation and coherence

Both clearly show a success symbol and a bordered reservation summary with workshop, session, quantity, and name. P leads with the person's confirmation and repeats included materials in the summary. Q leads with a larger celebratory headline, then the person's name; its summary is leaner. Cancellation is appropriately outlined rather than filled in both, though the combined cancellation-and-choose-again label makes it the only visible next path.

Q's navy, square outlined controls, heavy headlines, and blue top rules remain visibly consistent across all states. P's catalogue uses a softer, heavier card-heading treatment, while its form and confirmation text appear thinner and somewhat different in rendering; the shared green, cream, borders, and spacing still tie the states together. This is a visible impression only, not a diagnosis of fonts or capture behavior.

Q discloses the session-only demo prominently and consistently in a lime banner, but that high-attention implementation message competes with the workshop task. P integrates demo information more quietly and explains the reservation limitation thoroughly at confirmation. Q's confirmation states that no payment or real booking occurred especially directly; P's materials reminder is more useful for remembering the workshop.

## Tradeoff

P favors calm browsing, conspicuous catalogue actions, explicit form guidance, and a richer confirmation. Q favors stronger visual identity, denser mobile comparison, useful availability metadata, and consistent styling across states. Neither is an unconditional winner: P would benefit from less mobile card height and more consistent state typography; Q would benefit from quieter demo messaging and stronger visual emphasis on catalogue actions and invalid fields.
